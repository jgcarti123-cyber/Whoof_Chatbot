# Chocolate Toxicity in Dogs

## Why chocolate is dangerous

Chocolate contains theobromine (and to a lesser extent caffeine), methylxanthines that dogs metabolise far more slowly than humans. Theobromine stimulates the central nervous system and cardiovascular system, and at high enough doses causes seizures, cardiac arrhythmias, and death.

## Toxic amounts by chocolate type

Theobromine content varies widely:

| Type | Theobromine (approx.) | Risk threshold (for a 10 kg dog) |
|---|---|---|
| White chocolate | ~0.25 mg/g | Very low — mainly risk from fat/sugar |
| Milk chocolate | ~1.5–2 mg/g | Mild signs: ~115g; severe: ~230g |
| Dark chocolate (70%) | ~8–12 mg/g | Mild signs: ~25g; severe: ~55g |
| Baking/unsweetened chocolate | ~16–22 mg/g | Severe signs: ~14g |
| Cocoa powder | ~28 mg/g | Severe signs: ~10g |

**Key point:** For a large dog (30 kg Labrador), a small amount of milk chocolate may cause only mild stomach upset. For a small dog (5 kg), even a small square of dark chocolate can cause serious toxicity. Always err on the side of calling a vet or the ASPCA poison control line.

## Clinical signs (onset 6-12 hours after ingestion)

**Mild (lower doses):**
- Vomiting
- Diarrhea
- Restlessness, hyperactivity

**Moderate:**
- Excessive thirst and urination
- Muscle tremors
- Panting, elevated heart rate

**Severe:**
- Seizures
- Cardiac arrhythmias
- Rigidity, hyperthermia
- Coma

## What to do

**If ingestion was within the last 1-2 hours and the dog is not yet showing severe symptoms:**
- Call your vet or an emergency clinic immediately. They may advise inducing vomiting (with hydrogen peroxide) or ask you to come in for the vet to induce vomiting safely.
- Do NOT induce vomiting at home if the dog is already having tremors or seizures.

**Bring this information to the vet:**
- Dog's weight
- Type of chocolate (milk, dark, baking)
- Approximate amount ingested
- Time of ingestion
- Current symptoms

**At the clinic:** Treatment may include activated charcoal, IV fluids, anti-arrhythmics, muscle relaxants, or anticonvulsants depending on severity.

## Bottom line

There is no "safe" amount of dark or baking chocolate. Even if the dog seems fine, call a vet whenever any chocolate ingestion occurs — early intervention is far more effective than treating established toxicity.

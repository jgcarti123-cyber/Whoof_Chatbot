# Foods That Are Toxic to Dogs

## Grapes and raisins

**High priority.** Grapes, raisins, sultanas, currants, and foods containing them (fruit cake, trail mix, grape juice) can cause acute kidney failure in dogs. The exact toxic compound is unknown, which makes dosing unpredictable — some dogs have reacted to a single grape.

Signs: vomiting and diarrhea within 6-12 hours, followed by lethargy, reduced urination, or no urination (indicating kidney shutdown) within 24-72 hours.

**Action:** Any grape/raisin ingestion is an emergency. Go to a vet immediately. Early decontamination and IV fluids can prevent kidney failure if treated quickly.

## Onions, garlic, and related alliums

Includes raw, cooked, powdered, or dehydrated onion and garlic. Leeks, chives, and shallots are also toxic. Toxic compounds (N-propyl disulfide) oxidise haemoglobin in red blood cells, causing haemolytic anaemia.

**Special note:** Garlic powder is more concentrated than fresh garlic — many homemade dog treat recipes that include "a pinch of garlic powder" are a problem. Toxic doses are relatively low for garlic (approximately 5 g/kg for fresh garlic).

Signs develop 1-5 days after ingestion: lethargy, weakness, pale or yellow-tinged gums, reduced appetite, vomiting, red-tinged urine.

Cats are more sensitive than dogs, but dogs are not immune.

## Macadamia nuts

Cause weakness, hyperthermia, vomiting, tremors, and inability to walk within 12 hours. Mechanism unknown. Usually self-limiting but uncomfortable; see a vet if symptoms are severe.

## Avocado

The leaves, bark, skin, and pit contain persin. The fleshy part has lower persin levels and is less dangerous for dogs, but the pit is a choking/obstruction hazard and contains the most persin. In practice, an occasional small amount of avocado flesh is unlikely to cause severe toxicity, but it is best avoided.

## Alcohol

Even small amounts can cause serious harm — vomiting, disorientation, dangerous drops in blood sugar and body temperature, respiratory depression, coma. Never give any alcohol intentionally.

## Caffeine (beyond chocolate)

Coffee, tea, energy drinks, caffeine tablets. Similar mechanism to theobromine. Signs: hyperactivity, restlessness, rapid breathing, tremors, seizures.

## Salt / sodium ion

Very high-salt foods (crisps, salted nuts, soy sauce) in large quantities can cause sodium ion poisoning — vomiting, diarrhea, excessive thirst, neurological signs.

## Cooked bones

Cooked chicken, lamb, or pork bones can splinter and cause intestinal perforation or obstruction. Raw bones under supervision are generally safer, but carry bacterial risks. Always supervise bone chewing.

## What to do

If you suspect ingestion of any of the above: note the type and estimated amount, the dog's weight, and the time of ingestion, then call your vet or an emergency clinic immediately. For confirmed toxic ingestion, don't wait for signs.

# Heat Exhaustion and Heatstroke in Dogs

## Why Indian summers are high risk

Dogs cannot sweat through their skin — they cool almost entirely by panting. In hot, humid conditions (common in much of India from March to June, and during festivals with crowds and car travel), this mechanism can be overwhelmed quickly. Heatstroke is a genuine emergency with a high fatality rate if not treated within minutes to hours.

## Risk factors

- Brachycephalic (flat-faced) breeds: Pugs, Bulldogs, Shih Tzus, French Bulldogs — their narrow airways make panting less efficient
- Overweight dogs
- Thick double coats (Huskies, Samoyeds)
- Puppies and elderly dogs
- Exercise in the middle of the day
- Being left in a parked car — even with windows slightly open, car interiors can reach 50°C within 10 minutes on a 35°C day

## Signs of heat exhaustion (early — act now before it becomes heatstroke)

- Heavy, rapid panting that won't settle
- Excessive drooling (thick, ropy saliva)
- Bright red or dark red gums and tongue
- Seeking cool surfaces, restless or disoriented
- Weakness in hind legs

## Signs of heatstroke (emergency — get to vet immediately)

- Staggering, collapse
- Vomiting or diarrhea (may contain blood)
- Glazed eyes, confusion, or seizure
- Gums turning pale, grey, or blue
- Loss of consciousness
- Body temperature above 40.5°C (104.9°F) if you have a thermometer

## First aid while getting to the vet

**Do this immediately:**
1. Move the dog to shade or an air-conditioned space.
2. Wet the dog's fur with cool (NOT ice cold) water — focus on paws, groin, armpits, neck. Ice water causes blood vessels to constrict and traps heat.
3. Place in front of a fan.
4. Offer small amounts of cool water to drink if conscious and able to swallow.
5. Drive to the nearest vet or emergency clinic without delay — cooling helps, but internal organ damage from heatstroke requires veterinary treatment.

**Do NOT:**
- Use ice packs or ice water directly on skin
- Force the dog to drink large quantities
- Delay transport to cool the dog at home

## Recovery and monitoring

Even if the dog appears to recover, a vet visit is essential. Heatstroke can cause delayed kidney failure, clotting disorders, and brain injury that are not apparent initially.

## Prevention

- Walk dogs before 8am or after 7pm in summer
- Always carry water on walks
- Never leave dogs in parked vehicles
- In extreme heat, exercise in short sessions with water breaks

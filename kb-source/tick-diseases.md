# Tick-Borne Diseases in Dogs — India

## Overview

Ticks are among the most clinically significant external parasites in India. Three tick-borne diseases are particularly common and should be on the differential list for any dog with unexplained fever, lethargy, low platelet count, or anaemia.

## Canine Monocytic Ehrlichiosis (CME)

**Pathogen:** Ehrlichia canis, transmitted by the brown dog tick (Rhipicephalus sanguineus)

**Clinical signs:**
- Fever, lethargy, weight loss
- Anorexia (not eating)
- Generalised lymph node enlargement
- Thrombocytopenia (low platelets → bleeding tendency: nosebleeds, blood in urine, bruising under the skin)
- Ocular discharge, uveitis
- In chronic cases: bone marrow suppression, severe anaemia

**Diagnosis:** Platelet count, specific serology (IFA), or PCR. A low platelet count in a febrile dog with tick exposure should prompt Ehrlichia testing.

**Treatment:** Doxycycline (first-line, 10 mg/kg/day for 4 weeks). Response is usually good if caught early; chronic cases carry a poorer prognosis.

## Canine Babesiosis

**Pathogen:** Babesia gibsoni (common in India) and Babesia canis; transmitted by Rhipicephalus ticks

**Clinical signs:**
- Haemolytic anaemia (the parasite lives and replicates inside red blood cells, destroying them)
- Pale or yellow (jaundiced) gums and eyes
- Fever, weakness, collapse
- Splenomegaly (enlarged spleen)
- Dark/red-tinged urine (haemoglobin from destroyed red blood cells)

**Diagnosis:** Blood smear (ring or piriform parasites visible inside RBCs), PCR for definitive speciation.

**Treatment:** Imidocarb dipropionate (injectable, vet-administered); supportive care including blood transfusions in severe anaemia.

## Canine Hepatozoonosis

**Pathogen:** Hepatozoon canis, acquired by the dog *ingesting* infected ticks (not from a tick bite)

**Clinical signs:** Often subclinical. In severe cases: fever, muscle wasting, pain, ocular discharge, hyperostosis (abnormal bone growth on X-ray).

**Diagnosis:** Blood smear showing gamonts inside white blood cells; PCR.

**Treatment:** No fully curative protocol; combination therapy (trimethoprim-sulfadiazine, clindamycin, pyrimethamine) reduces parasitaemia.

## Tick prevention — essential in India

Monthly topical spot-ons (imidacloprid + permethrin, fipronil) or tick collars (Seresto) significantly reduce tick burden. Check your dog daily for ticks, especially after walks in grass or forested areas. Remove ticks promptly with tweezers — grasp close to the skin and pull straight out without twisting; disinfect the bite site.

## Key takeaway

Any dog with unexplained fever, low platelet count, anaemia, or yellow-tinged mucous membranes in India should have tick-borne diseases on the differential diagnosis list. Many Indian dogs carry co-infections with more than one tick-borne pathogen.

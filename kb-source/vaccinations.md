# Canine Vaccination — Core and Non-Core Vaccines

## Core vaccines (all dogs, regardless of lifestyle)

The WSAVA defines the following as core vaccines for dogs worldwide:

**Distemper, Hepatitis, Parvovirus (and Parainfluenza) — DHPPi:**
- Puppy series: first dose at 6-8 weeks, then every 3-4 weeks until 16 weeks of age (minimum 3 doses). A final puppy dose at 14-16 weeks is critical because maternal antibodies can block earlier doses.
- Adult: booster at 1 year after the puppy series, then every 3 years (WSAVA guidelines support triennial boosters for most adult dogs; some vets still recommend annual).
- In India, given the high burden of parvovirus in urban areas, many practitioners recommend completing the full puppy series strictly on schedule.

**Rabies:**
- First dose: typically at 12-16 weeks (follow state/municipal guidelines — rabies vaccination is legally mandated in many Indian states).
- Booster: 1 year after the first dose, then every 1-3 years depending on local regulations and product used.
- There is no treatment for rabies once clinical signs appear. Vaccination is non-negotiable.

## Non-core vaccines (based on risk)

**Leptospirosis (Lepto 4):**
- Highly recommended for dogs in India, particularly in monsoon-prone regions and areas with standing water, rodent exposure, or farmland.
- 2-dose primary series 3-4 weeks apart, then annual boosters.
- Leptospirosis can infect humans (zoonotic) and is often underdiagnosed.

**Kennel Cough (Bordetella bronchiseptica ± canine parainfluenza):**
- Recommended for dogs that board, attend training classes, dog parks, or shows.
- Intranasal or oral vaccine preferred; injectable also available.
- Annual boosters or before planned exposure.

**Canine Influenza:**
- Less common in Indian clinical practice but considered for dogs with high social contact.

## Practical notes

- Wait at least 48-72 hours after vaccination before bathing; watch for local swelling, lethargy, or facial oedema (rare but possible allergic reaction — if facial swelling occurs, see a vet within 1-2 hours).
- Titer testing (measuring antibody levels) is an alternative to automatic re-vaccination in adult dogs and can guide whether boosters are truly needed.
- Puppies are not fully protected until 7-10 days after their final puppy dose — limit exposure to unvaccinated dogs and contaminated environments until then.

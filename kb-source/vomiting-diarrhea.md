# Vomiting and Diarrhea in Dogs

## Common causes of acute vomiting

Dietary indiscretion (eating garbage, spoiled food, or a sudden diet change) is the most common cause of acute vomiting in otherwise healthy dogs. Other frequent causes include:

- Gastritis (stomach irritation)
- Intestinal parasites (roundworms, hookworms, giardia)
- Parvovirus (especially in unvaccinated or incompletely vaccinated puppies — treat as an emergency)
- Pancreatitis (often after a fatty meal)
- Motion sickness
- Foreign body ingestion
- Toxin or medication reaction

## When to wait and watch vs. go to the vet immediately

**See a vet today (same day) if:**
- Vomiting more than 3-4 times in one hour, or persists beyond 24 hours
- Blood in the vomit (red or coffee-ground appearance)
- The dog is lethargic, weak, or won't stand
- Abdomen appears bloated, hard, or painful to touch
- Puppy under 16 weeks with any vomiting (parvovirus risk)
- Known or suspected ingestion of a toxin, medication, or foreign object
- Concurrent fever, pale gums, or collapse

**Reasonable to monitor at home for up to 12-24 hours if:**
- Dog is alert, interested in surroundings, and not in obvious distress
- Single episode of vomiting after eating grass or a dietary change
- No blood, no abdominal distension

## Home management for mild, uncomplicated vomiting

1. Withhold food for 4-6 hours (not water unless advised by vet; avoid dehydration).
2. Offer small amounts of water or unflavoured electrolyte solution frequently.
3. After the rest period, offer a bland diet: boiled plain chicken breast + plain white rice (1:3 ratio), small portions 3-4 times daily.
4. Gradually transition back to regular food over 3-5 days.

## Diarrhea

**Acute diarrhea** (less than 2-3 days, no blood, dog is bright and eating) is commonly dietary. Bland diet approach as above. Probiotics (e.g. FortiFlora) may help restore gut flora.

**See a vet if diarrhea:**
- Contains blood or is very dark/tarry (digested blood — upper GI bleed)
- Is profuse, watery, and frequent (risk of rapid dehydration)
- Is accompanied by vomiting, lethargy, or fever
- Persists beyond 48-72 hours despite bland diet
- Occurs in a puppy (can dehydrate fatally in hours)

## Dehydration check

Gently pinch the skin at the back of the neck and release. In a well-hydrated dog it springs back immediately. If it stays "tented" for more than 2 seconds, the dog is dehydrated and needs veterinary fluids.

# Xylitol Toxicity in Dogs

## What is xylitol?

Xylitol is a sugar alcohol used as a sweetener in many human products. It is safe for humans but extremely toxic to dogs — one of the most dangerous everyday household toxins, gram for gram.

## Common sources

- Sugar-free chewing gum (even 1-2 sticks can be dangerous for a small dog)
- Some peanut butters and nut butters marketed as "healthy" or "sugar-free" — **always check the label before giving peanut butter to dogs**
- Sugar-free baked goods, mints, candies
- Some vitamins and supplements (especially children's chewable vitamins)
- Dental hygiene products (some mouthwashes, toothpastes)
- Some medications (liquid formulations)
- Certain protein bars and "keto" snacks

## Why it is so dangerous

In dogs (unlike humans), xylitol triggers a rapid, massive release of insulin from the pancreas. This causes:

1. **Rapid hypoglycaemia (low blood sugar):** within 10-60 minutes of ingestion. Blood glucose can crash dangerously fast.
2. **Acute liver failure:** at higher doses, xylitol causes direct hepatocellular necrosis — liver failure that may not appear for 24-72 hours after exposure.

## Clinical signs

**Hypoglycaemia (within 30-60 minutes):**
- Vomiting
- Weakness, staggering
- Tremors, seizures
- Collapse, coma

**Liver failure (12-72 hours later):**
- Jaundice (yellow tinge to eyes/skin/gums)
- Vomiting
- Bruising, bleeding (clotting failure)
- Lethargy

## Toxic doses

Even small amounts are dangerous. The ASPCA lists the following rough thresholds:
- **Hypoglycaemia:** ≥0.1 g/kg body weight
- **Liver failure:** ≥0.5 g/kg body weight

Gum commonly contains 0.3-0.4 g xylitol per stick. For a 5 kg dog, 1-2 sticks of gum can cause hypoglycaemia.

## What to do — treat as an emergency

**Go to an emergency vet immediately.** Do not wait for signs to develop. Xylitol toxicity can progress from normal-looking to seizing within an hour.

- If ingestion was within 30 minutes and the dog is not yet symptomatic, the vet may induce vomiting and administer dextrose (glucose) supplementation.
- Hospitalisation with IV dextrose, monitoring of blood glucose and liver enzymes is standard treatment.
- Blood glucose and liver function should be checked for at least 24-72 hours.

Early treatment dramatically improves outcome. Delayed treatment often results in irreversible liver failure.
